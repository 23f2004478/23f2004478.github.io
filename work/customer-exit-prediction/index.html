Banking Customer Churn Prediction under Class Skew

Benchmarked 8 classifier families over 90,000 banking customer records. Managed missing values with IterativeImputer and tuned Random Forest to 0.6365 validation F1.

Context IIT Madras Machine Learning Practice
Timeline T2 2026
Dataset 90,000 Customer Records
Top Model Tuned Random Forest

The Business Context of Retail Banking Churn

Retaining existing banking customers is significantly more cost-effective than acquiring new accounts. However, customer attrition datasets exhibit sharp class imbalance, with typically less than 20% of customers closing their accounts in a given observation window.

When evaluated strictly on accuracy, a baseline model predicting zero churn achieves ~80% accuracy while failing to identify a single exiting customer. The modeling objective was building a robust classifier that maximizes F1 score across 10 financial and demographic indicators.

Data Preprocessing & Missing Value Imputation

The training corpus comprised 90,000 banking customer observations with features including credit score, geography, gender, age, tenure, account balance, product count, credit card ownership, active membership status, and estimated salary.

  • Categorical Fields: Handled missing categorical entries using mode imputation, followed by one-hot encoding.
  • Multivariate Numerical Imputation: Employed scikit-learn's IterativeImputer with BayesianRidge estimators, modeling each numerical feature as a function of all remaining attributes to preserve multivariate correlations.
  • Validation Strategy: Implemented a 5-fold stratified cross-validation split to maintain identical churn proportions across training and validation partitions.

Hyperparameter Optimization & Decision Analysis

Selected the top three performing models (Random Forest, LightGBM, and XGBoost) for systematic hyperparameter tuning using RandomizedSearchCV across 5-fold stratified splits.

The tuned Random Forest model achieved the highest balanced performance with an F1 score of 0.6365, achieving a precision of 0.6181 and a recall of 0.6561 at an overall accuracy of 0.8414. This balance ensures retention marketing campaigns effectively capture 65%+ of at-risk customers while avoiding unnecessary promotional spending on loyal accounts.

Key Analytical Insights

  • Accuracy masking: Evaluating models on raw accuracy leads to severe false-positive overconfidence in churn retention pipelines.
  • Imputation fidelity: Multivariate iterative imputation preserves feature distributions far more reliably than naive mean replacement.
  • Precision-recall trade-off: Operationalizing churn models requires calibrating threshold cutoffs against retention outreach unit economics.
